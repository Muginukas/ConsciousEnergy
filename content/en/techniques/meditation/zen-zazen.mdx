## The Posture

Zazen places significant emphasis on posture — it is not incidental but considered integral to the practice.

**Legs:** Full lotus, half lotus, or Burmese position. If these cause pain, use a seiza bench or sit on a chair with feet flat on the floor. Physical pain from bad posture is not helpful; physical stillness is the goal.

**Spine:** Erect and naturally curved — not rigid, not collapsed. The crown of the head presses gently skyward.

**Hands:** Form the cosmic mudra — place your left hand on top of your right, both facing upward, with thumbs lightly touching, forming an oval. Rest in the lap.

**Eyes:** Half-open, cast downward at roughly a 45° angle. Not closed. Not wide open. This is a distinctive Zen instruction.

**Chin:** Slightly tucked.

## The Practice

Once positioned:

1. Take a few settling breaths.

2. **Sit.** There is no technique, in the highest understanding. In practice, if the mind needs an anchor, count breaths from 1 to 10 and repeat. When you lose count, return to 1 without self-criticism.

3. When thoughts arise, neither chase them nor suppress them. Let them be like clouds moving through the sky of awareness. Return to sitting.

4. In more advanced practice, a teacher may give you a koan — a paradoxical question like "What is your original face before your parents were born?" — to hold in awareness during sitting.

**Kinhin (Walking Zazen):** Between periods of sitting, Zen practitioners do slow walking meditation — one step per breath — in a circle. This integrates the stillness of sitting into movement.

Traditional Zen sessions (sesshins) alternate 40-minute sits with kinhin and may last multiple days.

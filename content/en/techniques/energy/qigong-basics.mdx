## The Basic Standing Posture (Wu Ji)

1. **Stand with feet shoulder-width apart**, toes pointing slightly outward (or parallel).

2. **Bend the knees slightly** — just enough to feel a softening, as if you're about to sit but haven't. This unlocks the knee joint.

3. **Tuck the tailbone slightly** to lengthen the lower back — imagine a string gently pulling your tailbone down.

4. **Relax the chest** — let the chest hollow slightly, drawing the shoulder blades wide. No puffing out.

5. **Shoulders down, arms by the sides** — elbows slightly bent, palms facing backward or the thighs.

6. **Head floats upward** — as if suspended from above by a golden thread from the crown.

7. **Tongue to the palate** — rest the tip of your tongue gently behind your upper teeth. This closes the energy circuit.

8. **Eyes half-open** or closed — soft gaze downward.

## The Practice

Once in position:

1. Simply **stand**. The practice is to be still and present.

2. As discomfort builds (it will), observe it. Don't fight. Don't run. The discomfort teaches you where you hold tension unconsciously.

3. **Breathe naturally** through the nose. Belly breathing (the belly rises on inhale, falls on exhale).

4. After some minutes, you may feel warmth, tingling, pulsing, or a subtle electromagnetic field between your palms — this is often the first felt experience of Qi.

5. **Build duration gradually.** 5 min → 10 → 20. Masters practice 1–2 hours daily.

**Advanced variation:** Raise the arms to holding-ball position (as if embracing a large ball at chest height) — this is called "Embracing the Tree" and intensifies the Qi cultivation and physical demand.

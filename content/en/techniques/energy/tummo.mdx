## What is Tummo?

Tummo belongs to the Anuttarayoga Tantra — the highest yoga tantra of the Tibetan Buddhist tradition. It is one of the Six Yogas of Naropa, a complete set of inner practices for achieving enlightenment in one lifetime.

The word *tummo* literally means "fierce woman" or "savage" — a reference to the fierce wisdom that burns through ignorance and delusion.

## The Two Aspects

Tummo has two inseparable aspects:

**The Fire (Warmth):** The physiological heat generated through breath (especially vase breath / pot breathing), bandhas (energy locks), and visualization.

**The Emptiness (Bliss):** The recognition that the fire arises in the nature of mind itself — empty, luminous, aware. The fire is not separate from awareness.

## The Vase Breath (General Introduction)

This is one element of Tummo — the primary pranayama. It should be practiced under guidance.

1. Inhale deeply, drawing air into the lower belly.
2. Slightly contract the pelvic floor (lower lock) and the throat (upper lock) simultaneously.
3. The breath is held in the lower abdomen — the "vase" shape with belly extended.
4. Visualize an intensely bright flame at the navel center, about the size of a sesame seed.
5. On the retention, the flame grows fierce and hot.
6. Exhale slowly.

**Note:** This description is a general orientation, not a substitute for proper Vajrayana instruction. To practice Tummo fully, seek refuge, empowerment, and transmission from a qualified lineage teacher.

## What 'seed' means in spiritual traditions

The word 'seed' has several layers:

1. **Physical seed** — male semen; female eggs, menstrual blood and sexual fluids.
2. **Seed essence** — the life force that 'charges' the physical seed. Taoists call it **Jing (精)**, yogis **Bindu** or **Virya**, Ayurveda **Shukra** and its refined form **Ojas**.
3. **Creative power** — the same energy that can create a new life can be directed into health, creativity and the growth of consciousness.

The shared idea: **sexual energy is the most powerful energy available to a human being**. It can be released (outer creation — a child, pleasure), wasted (unconsciously, too often), or **transformed** — raised upward and turned into vitality, warmth of the heart and luminous awareness.

## Taoism: Jing, Qi, Shen

Taoist inner alchemy (Nei Dan) rests on the **Three Treasures**:

- **Jing** (essence) — seed, constitutional life force, the 'root'. Stored in the kidneys and the lower Dantian (below the navel).
- **Qi** (vital energy) — breath, movement, warmth. Middle Dantian (chest, heart).
- **Shen** (spirit) — awareness, light in the eyes, clarity. Upper Dantian (head, between the brows).

Jing is divided into:
- **Prenatal Jing** — inherited from the parents, limited like a battery's capacity. It can be conserved but is hard to replenish.
- **Postnatal Jing** — gained from food, breath and sleep. It supplements the prenatal.

The stages of alchemy:
1. **Lian Jing Hua Qi** — refine essence into energy (raising the seed, the orbit).
2. **Lian Qi Hua Shen** — refine energy into spirit.
3. **Lian Shen Huan Xu** — return spirit to the Void.
4. (**Lian Xu He Dao** — merge with the Tao.)

A famous Taoist principle is **'Huan Jing Bu Nao' (還精補腦)**: 'Return the essence to nourish the brain.' This is what raising the seed means: instead of letting the energy flow down and out, it is drawn up the spine (Du Mai channel) to the head, then brought down the front channel (Ren Mai) and stored at the navel. This loop is the **Microcosmic Orbit**.

Classic sources: the Mawangdui manuscripts (2nd c. BCE) 'Ten Questions' and 'Uniting Yin and Yang', the 'Su Nü Jing' (Classic of the Plain Girl), and physician Sun Simiao's (7th c.) 'Qianjin Yaofang'.

## Yoga and Tantra: Bindu, Virya, Urdhvareta

- **Bindu** ('point, drop') — the condensed essence of consciousness and life. The Hatha Yoga Pradipika (3.88): *'Death comes from the falling of bindu; life from its retention.'*
- The tradition holds that bindu arises in the head (Bindu Visarga, at the top-back of the skull) and drips downward, where it is 'consumed' in the sexual centre. Practices such as Khechari mudra, Vajroli mudra and Mula bandha aim to stop and reverse this flow.
- **Virya** — strength, courage, vigour. Patanjali's Yoga Sutras (II.38): *'When established in brahmacharya, virya is gained.'*
- **Brahmacharya** — literally 'walking in Brahman'; often translated as continence, its essence is the conscious mastery and redirection of sexual energy toward a spiritual goal.
- **Urdhvareta** — 'one whose seed flows upward'. The yogic ideal: sexual energy no longer leaves but rises through Sushumna as Kundalini.
- In Tantra, sexual union (maithuna) without releasing bindu is a path to the inner union of Shiva and Shakti.

## Ayurveda: Shukra and Ojas

Ayurveda describes seven tissues (dhatus), each formed from the previous: plasma → blood → muscle → fat → bone → marrow/nerve → **Shukra** (reproductive tissue). Shukra is the last and most refined tissue, so its formation 'costs' the work of the whole chain.

From the essence of Shukra comes **Ojas** — the substance of immunity, endurance, radiance and calm. With abundant Ojas a person is resilient, calm, with shining eyes; when it is depleted — anxiety, weakness, illness. Ojas gives rise to **Tejas** (inner fire, insight) and **Prana** (vital breath).

The popular claim that '40 drops of blood make one drop of semen' is symbolic — it conveys that the seed is precious, but it is not physiologically accurate.

## Other traditions

- **Kabbalah** — the sefirah Yesod (foundation, sexual power) and *Shmirat HaBrit* ('guarding the covenant') — the injunction not to waste seed.
- **Christian monasticism** — chastity as the gathering of energy for prayer.
- **19th-century America** — John Humphrey Noyes' 'Male Continence' (Oneida community) and Dr Alice Bunker Stockham's 'Karezza' (1896) — union without orgasm.
- **Napoleon Hill**, 'Think and Grow Rich' (1937) — chapter 'The Mystery of Sex Transmutation': sexual energy redirected into creative work.
- **The modern semen-retention / NoFap movement** — popularises abstinence but often exaggerates 'superpower' promises.

## Men and women

Taoists teach that **men** lose most Jing through **ejaculation** and **women** through **menstruation** (and childbirth). Hence:

- Men's practices: ejaculation control, testicle breathing, the Big Draw, orgasm without ejaculation.
- Women's practices: ovarian breathing, breast massage, the doe exercise, Sahajoli mudra; in advanced female alchemy, 'Slaying the Red Dragon' (transforming menstrual energy). For women, orgasm is generally not seen as energy loss — it can be raised upward.

## The core formula

> **Conserve → Raise → Circulate → Store → Refine**

1. **Conserve** — don't let the seed (and attention) leak needlessly.
2. **Raise** — use the pelvic floor, breath and attention to lift energy up the spine.
3. **Circulate** — run it through the Microcosmic Orbit so it doesn't pool in the head.
4. **Store** — end every practice by gathering energy in the lower Dantian (navel area).
5. **Refine** — over time the energy becomes subtler: warmth → light → stillness.

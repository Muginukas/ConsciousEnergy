## What is Kundalini?

In the yogic understanding, Kundalini Shakti is the primordial life-force energy — the same creative power that sustains the universe — present in every human being in a coiled, dormant form at the base of the spine (Muladhara chakra).

Most people live their entire lives with this energy predominantly inactive. Spiritual practice, grace from a teacher (shaktipat), or sometimes spontaneous life events can begin to awaken it.

## The Path of Awakening

The traditional model describes Kundalini rising through **three channels (nadis):**

- **Ida** — Left, feminine, lunar, cooling
- **Pingala** — Right, masculine, solar, activating
- **Sushumna** — Central, the royal highway of kundalini

The goal is to draw prana from Ida and Pingala into Sushumna, allowing Kundalini to rise through the central channel.

## As It Rises: The Chakras

As Kundalini moves through each chakra, the qualities of that chakra become more available — grounding and safety (Muladhara), creativity (Svadhisthana), personal power (Manipura), love (Anahata), expression (Vishuddha), vision (Ajna), and ultimately union (Sahasrara).

This process is not linear, smooth, or necessarily comfortable. It may produce intense physical sensations (heat, trembling, spontaneous movement), emotional releases, visionary experiences, or periods of profound stillness.

## Signs of Kundalini Activity

Common signs include: involuntary body movements (kriyas), experiences of heat rising in the spine, intense energy in the body, states of expanded awareness, increased sensitivity, spontaneous insights, and sometimes periods of difficulty or disorientation.

## The Essential Role of a Teacher

The guidance of a qualified teacher who has themselves undergone this process is not a luxury — it is a necessity. The teacher can recognize what is happening, provide appropriate practices, and support safe integration.
